Robot Choir

One voice goes in. Its band envelopes shape a choir of four to eight drifting saw voices, spread across the stereo field and set in a plate reverb. Speech comes out sung.

Uncanny cues

Switch these off to hear the machine. Each cue a person makes without knowing is one a synthetic choir lacks.

Room

Plate reverb, a Dattorro-style design. Longer decay makes a longer render.

How it works

Analyzer: the speech passes through a bank of band filters on an ERB scale. Each output is rectified and smoothed into an envelope, as in Dudley's channel vocoder. A tracker supplies pitch and a voiced or unvoiced flag.

Synthesizer: each choir voice is a PolyBLEP saw with its own analog-style drift. Voices are placed left to right, then sum into a left and a right carrier. Each carrier goes through its own copy of the filter bank, with gains set by the same envelopes.

Checked and not checked

Checked by measurement in headless Chromium: more voices make a denser spectrum, spread decorrelates left and right, reverb adds a tail, and the output envelope still follows the speech.

Not checked: how it sounds, whether the words stay intelligible, and whether the cues read as human. The Dattorro plate coefficients were written from memory and not checked against the paper.
